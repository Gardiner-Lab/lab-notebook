How to use the Lab Notebook

A notebook is worth keeping when someone else — including you, in two years — can read a day and repeat what was done. This page covers how the notebook is arranged, writing a day, the blocks a page is built from, protocols, linking to plasmids, stocks and seeds, sharing, and finding things again. Golden rules

  1. Write on the day, not at the end of the week. Today opens today's page in one press. What you did not write down, you did not do.
  2. Link, don't retype. Type @ to link a plasmid, a stock, a seed lot or another page. The link stays right when things are renamed, and the other record then shows this page in return.
  3. Say what you actually did, including the part that went wrong. A failed day that says why is worth more than a tidy one that hides it.
  4. Put numbers in a table, not in a sentence. A table can do the arithmetic, scale a mix by the number of reactions, and be read later.
  5. Use a protocol for anything done twice. Bring it into the day with Protocol…; the day keeps the version you used, so improving the protocol later does not rewrite your history.
  6. One page, one piece of work. Keep a day page for the day, and put a long experiment on its own page underneath it.
  7. Nothing is lost. Every save keeps the version before it, and a deleted page can be brought back. There is no reason to keep a second copy somewhere else.

How it is arranged

Everything is a page, and pages sit inside pages, like folders that can also be written in. Your notebook fills itself as you go:

your notebook→ 2026→ October 2026→ 30-10-2026 Friday

Days, months and years are made for you the first time you write on a day, and are listed newest first. Anything else you make yourself: a page for a long experiment, a folder for a project.

Opening a page that has pages under it shows them as cards below it, with the start of each one, so a month shows its days and a project shows its experiments without clicking through.

Besides your notebook there is the Protocols library, which the whole lab shares.

Writing a day

  1. Press Today. Today's page opens, filed under its month and year, named 30-10-2026 Friday. Pressing it again later opens the same page.
  2. Write what you are doing as you do it. The page saves itself a second after you stop typing; the header says when it was last saved.
  3. Use Add: at the bottom to put in a table, steps, a gel photo or a protocol.
  4. A day with a booking shows it at the top, so the page knows which microscope you were on.

For an experiment that runs over several days, make a page of its own (+ on your notebook) and link to it from each day. Keep the day for what happened that day.

The week's to-do list

Each week of your notebook can have one to-do list, a page of its own that shows under every day page of that week: a task added or ticked on Tuesday is there on Thursday too.

  1. On any day page, press ＋ Week's to-do. The list for that week (Monday to Sunday) is made, with any tasks moved on to it from last week, and put under each day of the week; a day you start later in the week gets it too. On a week that has one, the button opens it.
  2. Type a task; Enter starts the next, Backspace in an empty one removes it. Type @ to tag a plasmid, a stock, a seed lot, a track or a page, as in any text: the to-do list then shows on that record as linking to it.
  3. Tick a task when it is done: the tick says who and when.
  4. → Next week moves a task still to do on to next week's list. It stays on this week's, greyed out and marked as moved, and is on next week's list as soon as that is made, saying which week it came from. Bring back undoes it, while it is not ticked there.

Whoever can change the day page can change its week's list; those who can only read it see it as it is. A day page that is deleted takes the list with it only when no other day of the week shows it.

Meetings

A meeting, once or repeating, gets a page of its own under the day it falls on, in your notebook and in the notebook of everyone you invite. It is one page you all write on: the agenda, who came, notes and action items.

  1. Open the Meetings tab at the top (or the Meetings button under Today): your week of meetings on a schedule, a column per day, with ‹ and › to the weeks before and after. Press ＋ New meeting there (or drag over the time on a day, as in Booking, to start one at that time; the whole day is there, from 00:00 to 24:00, opened at the hours of the week's meetings), or ＋ Meeting on a day page to set one up on that day. Click a meeting to open its page; a page for a week further ahead is made when you open it.
  2. Give it a title, the first date, the time, where it is and an agenda (one point per line: on each page every point gets a box of its own for its minutes), and choose how often it repeats: once, every week, every two or four weeks, or every month on the same weekday (the second Tuesday, the last Friday). A repeating meeting can stop at a last date, or go on until you end it.
  3. Tick the people to invite: anyone with an account in Trellis Tools, whatever their lab. Each meeting's page is shared with them, and shows under their day as well as yours.
  4. The pages of this week's and next week's meetings are made as soon as you, or anyone invited, opens the notebook; each week the next one follows, as the to-do list does.
  5. On the page, tick who came under Attendance (the tick says who ticked it and when), write under each point of the agenda what was said and decided on it (its minutes) in a box of your own, named, beside everyone else's, so people taking minutes at once never write over each other; write anything else under Notes, press Agenda point to add a point during the meeting, or Agenda item… to put a task or work package of one of your projects on the agenda (see Projects), and put what is to be done under Action items, where @ tags a plasmid, a stock, a seed lot, a track or a page.

→ My to-do beside an action item puts it on your to-do list of this week, saying which meeting it came from, with a link back. The meeting's page then says whose to-do list it is on, with a ✓ once ticked there.

A repeating meeting carries its action items on: the day after a meeting, the action items not ticked off go onto the next meeting's page, saying which meeting they came from, and stay on the earlier page marked as carried. Anything added to the earlier page later follows too, and an item still open at the next meeting is carried on again. Tick it on the newest page it is on.

Everyone invited takes notes on the meeting's page, as on any page shared with them. Only whoever set the meeting up changes the meeting itself: its time, place, agenda, how often it repeats and who is invited. They change it from the meeting's page (Change meeting) or from the Meetings list. A change reaches the pages from today on: a page nobody has written on is made again as the meeting now is; a page already written on keeps what was written, and gets the new title and the people added or taken off. Pages of days gone by stay as they were. To skip one date, delete its page: it is not made again (it comes back from Deleted like any page). End meeting stops it after today.

Projects

The Projects tab plans work: a project has work packages, each with tasks, and tasks may have subtasks. A Gantt chart of it all is drawn as you go.

  1. Press ＋ New project, and tick the people to invite: anyone in Trellis Tools. Every member adds and changes work packages and tasks; whoever set it up also renames it, invites and takes people off (Members), and deletes it.
  2. Add a work package, then its tasks with ＋ Task, and subtasks with ＋ beside a task. A task has a start, a length in days, and the people working on it; a task with subtasks spans them.
  3. Under Waits for, tick the tasks that must finish first. It then starts the day after the last of them finishes, plus a lag of days if you give one (2 for seeds to dry). Its subtasks wait with it.
  4. Change when a task finishes, by its length or start, or tick it done earlier or later than planned (Done on), and every task waiting for it moves, and those waiting for them: a message says which. A loop of tasks waiting for each other is refused.
  5. Click a task to open its page: write its description and notes there, with tables, steps, photos and @ links to protocols, stocks, seeds and plasmids, as on any page, with its history.
  6. Type @ anywhere you write, in text, a to-do list, action items or a table, and choose A task of a project to tag one of your projects' tasks. It shows as a link that opens the task, and the line you tagged it in shows on the task's page under Talked about.
  7. On a meeting's page press Agenda item…, or on any day or page of your notebook Task…, and choose a task or work package of your projects. It goes on the page as a section: what you write in it shows on the task's page under Talked about (and under Talked about… of a work package), with the meeting and its date.

⤓ Export chart, above the Gantt chart, saves it as a PDF (the chart on a landscape page, then a table of the tasks with their dates, what they wait for, who works on them and when they were done), a PNG picture for slides, or an SVG drawing that stays sharp at any size, for papers and posters.

Days are calendar days, weekends included. Deleting a task takes its subtasks and its page with it; what was written about it on meeting pages and days stays there.

Writing and formatting

Select text and the formatting bar appears above it:

  • B, I, U and S for bold, italic, underline and strike-through.
  • A opens the colours for the letters; ⌀ takes a colour off again.
  • The marker ✎ switches on and off like a real one: while it is on, what you type is highlighted. With text selected it highlights that text, or clears it if it is highlighted already. ▾ picks the colour, and ⌀ No highlight takes highlighting off.
  • H1, H2, H3 for headings, ¶ for ordinary text, and bulleted or numbered lists.
  • @ links a plasmid, page, stock or seed lot — the same as typing @.
  • 🔗 (or Ctrl+K) links the selected text to a web page or an email address, or changes or removes the link already there. Clicking a link opens it in a new tab.

Start a line with - (a dash and a space) and it becomes a bulleted list as you type the next character; start it with 1. and it becomes a numbered list.

Pasting keeps web links: copy from a web page, an email or another page and the links come with the text (its fonts and colours do not). An address pasted on its own becomes a link, and pasted over selected text it links that text.

Tab indents a list item and puts in a tab anywhere else, as in a word processor.

Tables

Add: ▦ Table makes a table that can do arithmetic — a reaction mix, a set of measurements, a scoring sheet.

  1. Click a column's name in the header to rename it.
  2. ⚙ Columns sets what each column is: text, a number, a formula, one scaled by reactions, one to fill in on the day, or a tick off box that records who ticked it and when.
  3. Formulas start with =. Name a column on its own (=B*C) for the same row, a cell as A1, a range as A1:A5. N is the number of reactions, and SUM, MIN, MAX, AVERAGE, ROUND, ABS and IF all work.
  4. Scale by reactions adds a box at the top: type 6 and a mix for one reaction becomes a mix for six.

A table copied from Excel, Google Sheets, LibreOffice or Word can be pasted straight in. Pasted into the text it becomes a table where you pasted it, keeping its rows and columns; pasted into a cell, it fills the table from there, adding rows and columns as needed.

Steps

Add: Steps is a list you tick off as you go, each tick keeping who ticked it and when. Use it for a procedure you are following now, where a protocol would be too much. A long step wraps onto more lines, so nothing is hidden.

To put a step in another place, drag the ⋮⋮ at its left to where it should go; a line shows where it will land. From the keyboard, Alt+↑ and Alt+↓ move the step you are in. A step keeps its tick when it moves.

Gel photos

  1. Add: ▥ Gel photo… and choose the photo, or pick one already on the page.
  2. Click each well you used, left to right. Each gets a number on the picture.
  3. Say what was in each lane in the table beside it. The numbers stay on the photo, so a figure taken from the notebook explains itself.

Photos and files

Drag photos and files onto a page, paste a screenshot straight in, or use Attach…. They are listed under Attachments and kept inside the notebook, so they are backed up with it.

Click one and it opens in the notebook itself, over the page: photos, PDFs, and Word, PowerPoint, Excel and text files (office files are shown as a PDF). ‹ › or the arrow keys step through the page's other attachments, ⤓ downloads the original, ↗ opens it in a tab of its own, and Esc closes it.

A photo can be marked up — drawn and written on — from its menu. The original is kept; the marked-up copy is a new attachment you can change again later.

3D models and scans

Attach a 3D model — GLB, glTF, USDZ (an iPhone or iPad scan) or PLY — or a Gaussian splat (3DGS: PLY, SPZ, SPLAT, KSPLAT, SOG) and click it to open it in 3D. Drag to turn it, right-drag or Shift-drag to move it, scroll to zoom. A scan that comes in lying down or upside down is stood up with Tip forward, Tip sideways and Flip; Reset shows all of it again.

Video… records it turning round, starting from the view on the screen: choose the size, the length and how far it turns, set up the view inside the frame, and press Record. The video is saved as an MP4 (a WebM in Firefox), ready for a talk or a paper's supplement. ▶ Turn shows the turn first, and Picture saves the view as a PNG.

A USDZ can be turned into a GLB, which more programs open: ⤓ GLB saves it, and Attach GLB puts it on the page beside the USDZ. A file can be up to 50 MB: a large splat is much smaller as an SPZ.

Links to the rest of the lab

Type @ anywhere in the text and pick what you mean:

  • a plasmid, which opens in the Sequence Editor;
  • a stock from Lab Stocks — a glycerol stock, primer, chemical or antibody;
  • a seed lot, line or plant from the Seed Bank;
  • a genome track from the Genome Browser, which opens its genome with the track on;
  • another page of the notebook.

Links work both ways: the stock or seed lot you link lists this page under Mentioned in. That is what lets a tube in a freezer show what was done with it. A track lists it under Details, In the Lab Notebook.

Add: MoClo reaction… brings a reaction from the MoClo Library Tool into the page, with its parts and volumes as a table.

Protocols

The Protocols library is shared by the lab: one copy of each method, improved over time.

  1. Write a protocol as a page in the library, with its steps and tables.
  2. In a day page, Add: Protocol… and choose it. A copy of it is placed in the day, marked with the version it came from.
  3. Tick its steps and fill in its fill in on the day cells as you work. What you write stays in the day; the library's protocol is untouched.
  4. Improving the protocol later does not change the days that already used it.

On a protocol, ＋ Add to page… (also in the library list) asks where to put it. Add to today is the first choice; or search for any page or day of yours. The protocol becomes a page of its own under that one, named for the protocol, with its steps ready to tick, so a day that uses several protocols stays easy to read. It says which version it came from, and the protocol lists it under Used on.

Star a protocol to keep it in Favourite protocols on the left.

Labels and finding things

The search box searches titles and the whole text of every page you may read, including protocols.

Labels sort pages across the tree: press ＋ label and add, say, #project=GFP or #strain=GV3101. Searching #project=GFP then finds every page of that project whatever day it is filed under. Days, months and years carry their own label with their date.

Sharing

  • Who reads whose notebook follows the lab's roles, unless a page is shared:
    • a lab admin reads every notebook in their lab, the lab leads' too, but not another lab admin's (someone who is lab admin of any lab) unless it is shared;
    • a lab lead reads every notebook in their lab except a lab admin's;
    • a member reads only their own.
    Only the owner writes in their notebook. An administrator of the suite reads them all, and a lab's leads read the notebook of someone who has left, so that their work is not lost with them; a lab admin's, only in the lab they were lab admin of.
  • Share on a page opens it to a person or a whole lab, to read or to write.
  • The Protocols library is shared with the lab as a matter of course.

A page someone shared with you appears under their name in the tree, and says whether you may change it.

History and export

  • History shows every saved version of a page, with who saved it, and lets you read or restore one.
  • Export makes a PDF to keep, print or send, or a Word document to edit. Both can take everything underneath the page with them, so a whole project or a whole year goes in one file. Photos are in the text, an attached PDF's pages are placed where it is attached, and other files travel with it.

Fixing mistakes

  • Wrote on the wrong day: cut the blocks and paste them into the right page, or use the ⋯ menu to move the page.
  • Deleted a page by accident: Deleted at the bottom left lists what was deleted, and brings it back.
  • Started a day, month or year by a typo (a year typed as 6, or 6202): open it, then ⋯ › Delete. A year or month goes with the days under it, to Deleted, so it can be brought back. A day with a booking on it stays until the booking is cancelled in Booking, as the notebook would only make it again.
  • Saved something wrong: open History and restore the version before it.
  • Two people editing at once: the notebook says so rather than overwriting, and shows you what the other person saved.
  • A link points at the wrong thing: delete it and type @ again. Renaming the plasmid or stock it points at does not break it.
  • A page is in the wrong place: drag it in the tree. A page can also sit in two places at once without being copied.
