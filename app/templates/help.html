How to use the Lab Notebook

A notebook is worth keeping when someone else — including you, in two years — can read a day and repeat what was done. This page covers how the notebook is arranged, writing a day, the blocks a page is built from, protocols, linking to plasmids, stocks and seeds, sharing, and finding things again. Golden rules

  1. Write on the day, not at the end of the week. Start today opens today's page in one press. What you did not write down, you did not do.
  2. Link, don't retype. Type @ to link a plasmid, a stock, a seed lot or another page. The link stays right when things are renamed, and the other record then shows this page in return.
  3. Say what you actually did, including the part that went wrong. A failed day that says why is worth more than a tidy one that hides it.
  4. Put numbers in a table, not in a sentence. A table can do the arithmetic, scale a mix by the number of reactions, and be read later.
  5. Use a protocol for anything done twice. Bring it into the day with Protocol…; the day keeps the version you used, so improving the protocol later does not rewrite your history.
  6. One page, one piece of work. Keep a day page for the day, and put a long experiment on its own page underneath it.
  7. Nothing is lost. Every save keeps the version before it, and a deleted page can be brought back. There is no reason to keep a second copy somewhere else.

How it is arranged

Everything is a page, and pages sit inside pages, like folders that can also be written in. Your notebook fills itself as you go:

your notebook→ 2026→ October 2026→ 30-10-2026 Friday

Days, months and years are made for you the first time you write on a day, and are listed newest first. Anything else you make yourself: a page for a long experiment, a folder for a project.

Opening a page that has pages under it shows them as cards below it, with the start of each one, so a month shows its days and a project shows its experiments without clicking through.

Besides your notebook there is the Protocols library, which the whole lab shares.

Writing a day

  1. Press Start today. Today's page opens, filed under its month and year, named 30-10-2026 Friday. Pressing it again later opens the same page.
  2. Write what you are doing as you do it. The page saves itself a second after you stop typing; the header says when it was last saved.
  3. Use Add: at the bottom to put in a table, steps, a gel photo or a protocol.
  4. A day with a booking shows it at the top, so the page knows which microscope you were on.

For an experiment that runs over several days, make a page of its own (+ on your notebook) and link to it from each day. Keep the day for what happened that day.

Writing and formatting

Select text and the formatting bar appears above it:

  • B, I, U and S for bold, italic, underline and strike-through.
  • A opens the colours for the letters; ⌀ takes a colour off again.
  • The marker ✎ switches on and off like a real one: while it is on, what you type is highlighted. With text selected it highlights that text, or clears it if it is highlighted already. ▾ picks the colour, and ⌀ No highlight takes highlighting off.
  • H1, H2, H3 for headings, ¶ for ordinary text, and bulleted or numbered lists.
  • @ links a plasmid, page, stock or seed lot — the same as typing @.

Tab indents a list item and puts in a tab anywhere else, as in a word processor.

Tables

Add: ▦ Table makes a table that can do arithmetic — a reaction mix, a set of measurements, a scoring sheet.

  1. Click a column's name in the header to rename it.
  2. ⚙ Columns sets what each column is: text, a number, a formula, one scaled by reactions, one to fill in on the day, or a tick off box that records who ticked it and when.
  3. Formulas start with =. Name a column on its own (=B*C) for the same row, a cell as A1, a range as A1:A5. N is the number of reactions, and SUM, MIN, MAX, AVERAGE, ROUND, ABS and IF all work.
  4. Scale by reactions adds a box at the top: type 6 and a mix for one reaction becomes a mix for six.

A table copied from Excel, Google Sheets, LibreOffice or Word can be pasted straight in. Pasted into the text it becomes a table where you pasted it, keeping its rows and columns; pasted into a cell, it fills the table from there, adding rows and columns as needed.

Steps

Add: Steps is a list you tick off as you go, each tick keeping who ticked it and when. Use it for a procedure you are following now, where a protocol would be too much. A long step wraps onto more lines, so nothing is hidden.

To put a step in another place, drag the ⋮⋮ at its left to where it should go; a line shows where it will land. From the keyboard, Alt+↑ and Alt+↓ move the step you are in. A step keeps its tick when it moves.

Gel photos

  1. Add: ▥ Gel photo… and choose the photo, or pick one already on the page.
  2. Click each well you used, left to right. Each gets a number on the picture.
  3. Say what was in each lane in the table beside it. The numbers stay on the photo, so a figure taken from the notebook explains itself.

Photos and files

Drag photos and files onto a page, paste a screenshot straight in, or use Attach…. They are listed under Attachments and kept inside the notebook, so they are backed up with it.

Click one and it opens in the notebook itself, over the page: photos, PDFs, and Word, PowerPoint, Excel and text files (office files are shown as a PDF). ‹ › or the arrow keys step through the page's other attachments, ⤓ downloads the original, ↗ opens it in a tab of its own, and Esc closes it.

A photo can be marked up — drawn and written on — from its menu. The original is kept; the marked-up copy is a new attachment you can change again later.

Links to the rest of the lab

Type @ anywhere in the text and pick what you mean:

  • a plasmid, which opens in the Plasmid Editor;
  • a stock from Lab Stocks — a glycerol stock, primer, chemical or antibody;
  • a seed lot, line or plant from the Seed Bank;
  • another page of the notebook.

Links work both ways: the stock or seed lot you link lists this page under Mentioned in. That is what lets a tube in a freezer show what was done with it.

Add: MoClo reaction… brings a reaction from the MoClo Library Tool into the page, with its parts and volumes as a table.

Protocols

The Protocols library is shared by the lab: one copy of each method, improved over time.

  1. Write a protocol as a page in the library, with its steps and tables.
  2. In a day page, Add: Protocol… and choose it. A copy of it is placed in the day, marked with the version it came from.
  3. Tick its steps and fill in its fill in on the day cells as you work. What you write stays in the day; the library's protocol is untouched.
  4. Improving the protocol later does not change the days that already used it.

On a protocol, ＋ Add to page… (also in the library list) asks where to put it. Add to today is the first choice; or search for any page or day of yours. The protocol becomes a page of its own under that one, named for the protocol, with its steps ready to tick, so a day that uses several protocols stays easy to read. It says which version it came from, and the protocol lists it under Used on.

Star a protocol to keep it in Favourite protocols on the left.

Labels and finding things

The search box searches titles and the whole text of every page you may read, including protocols.

Labels sort pages across the tree: press ＋ label and add, say, #project=GFP or #strain=GV3101. Searching #project=GFP then finds every page of that project whatever day it is filed under. Days, months and years carry their own label with their date.

Sharing

  • Your notebook is yours by default: nobody else reads it.
  • Share on a page opens it to a person or a whole lab, to read or to write.
  • A lab lead can be given the whole notebook of someone in their lab.
  • The Protocols library is shared with the lab as a matter of course.

A page someone shared with you appears under their name in the tree, and says whether you may change it.

History and export

  • History shows every saved version of a page, with who saved it, and lets you read or restore one.
  • Export makes a PDF to keep, print or send, or a Word document to edit. Both can take everything underneath the page with them, so a whole project or a whole year goes in one file. Photos are in the text, an attached PDF's pages are placed where it is attached, and other files travel with it.

Fixing mistakes

  • Wrote on the wrong day: cut the blocks and paste them into the right page, or use the ⋯ menu to move the page.
  • Deleted a page by accident: Deleted at the bottom left lists what was deleted, and brings it back.
  • Started a day, month or year by a typo (a year typed as 6, or 6202): open it, then ⋯ › Delete. A year or month goes with the days under it, to Deleted, so it can be brought back. A day with a booking on it stays until the booking is cancelled in Booking, as the notebook would only make it again.
  • Saved something wrong: open History and restore the version before it.
  • Two people editing at once: the notebook says so rather than overwriting, and shows you what the other person saved.
  • A link points at the wrong thing: delete it and type @ again. Renaming the plasmid or stock it points at does not break it.
  • A page is in the wrong place: drag it in the tree. A page can also sit in two places at once without being copied.
