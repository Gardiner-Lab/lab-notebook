What it holds

Days

A page for each day, filed under its month and year without you arranging anything. Start today opens today's in one press.

Pages

Text, headings, colours and a marker, photos, and pages inside pages for a long experiment or a project.

Active tables

Tables that do the arithmetic: formulas, a mix scaled by the number of reactions, cells to fill in on the day, and boxes to tick off that record who ticked them.

Protocols

A library the whole lab shares. A day takes a copy of the version it used, so improving a protocol never rewrites the past.

Gel photos

Click the wells you used and name what was in each. The numbers stay on the picture, so a figure taken from the notebook explains itself.

Attachments

Photos and files kept inside the notebook. Documents open in the page itself rather than downloading, and a photo can be drawn on.

How it is arranged

Everything is a page, and pages sit inside pages. Days fill themselves in as you write:

your notebook→ 2026→ October 2026→ 30-10-2026 Friday

A page may sit in more than one place without being copied, so one experiment can be filed under both its day and its project. Labels (#project=GFP) cut across the tree, and opening a page shows cards of the pages under it.

With the other tools

  • Sequence Editor. Type @ to link a plasmid; it opens there with its map and sequencing reads.
  • Lab Stocks. Link a glycerol stock, primer, chemical or antibody. The stock then lists the pages that mention it, so a tube shows what was done with it.
  • Seed Bank. Link a line, seed lot or plant, with the same two-way effect.
  • MoClo Library Tool. Bring a reaction into a page, with its parts and volumes as a table.
  • Booking. A day shows the equipment booked that day, so the page knows which microscope you were on.

Who sees what

  • Your notebook is yours. Nobody else reads it unless you share it.
  • Share a page with a person or a whole lab, to read or to write.
  • A lab lead can be given the whole notebook of someone in their lab.
  • The Protocols library is shared with the lab.

Nothing is lost

Every save keeps the version before it, with who saved it, and any version can be read or restored. A deleted page goes to Deleted and can be brought back; attachments stay with the versions that showed them. A page can be exported as a PDF or a Word document, with everything underneath it, so a project or a year leaves in one file.

Built on Trilium's design

Its structure follows Trilium Notes (TriliumNext; originally by zadam), a hierarchical note-taking application: content is kept in notes, and where a note sits in the tree is kept apart from it, so one note can appear in several places; notes carry labels and relations; every change is kept as a revision; and days are filed under their year and month as in Trilium's journal. The Lab Notebook is a separate program written for the lab suite, with its own code; it shares Trilium's ideas, not its source.
